Orbit

Privacy Policy

Effective November 1, 2026 · Operated by Social Connectors

Orbit is a private contacts app. It stores your data in an encrypted database on your own devices. We have no server that receives your data, and Orbit contains no advertising or third-party analytics code.

What Orbit reads

Orbit reads only what you give it permission to read:

  • Contacts, calendars and photos on your device.
  • On a Mac: Messages, call history, Notes, Find My, and the WhatsApp and Signal desktop apps.
  • Files you export from other services and import into Orbit, such as Instagram, LinkedIn, Facebook, Snapchat, X, TikTok, Discord, Telegram and Pinterest.
  • Accounts you connect: Google, iCloud Mail (message headers only), Slack, GroupMe, Eventbrite and Telegram.

Google data Orbit reads

When you connect a Google account, Orbit asks for read-only access to the Google services you leave turned on:

  • Gmail: each message’s sender, recipients, subject, date and text, up to 10,000 characters per message. Orbit does not download attachments, except calendar invites, which it adds to your calendar in Orbit.
  • Google Contacts: names, email addresses, phone numbers and organisations.
  • Google Calendar: your events.
  • Your Google account: your email address, to show which account is connected.

Orbit never sends, changes or deletes anything in your Google account.

How Orbit uses it

Orbit uses this data to keep your private contacts list up to date, to let you search your messages and email, and to answer questions you ask about the people you know.

If you turn on automatic re-import for a service, Orbit looks in Gmail for that service’s data-export emails, checks that they really come from that service, downloads the export file from the service’s own website, and imports it.

Storage and sync

  • Orbit stores your data in an encrypted database on your device.
  • If you pair several devices, Orbit syncs your data between them, encrypted on your device first, directly or through your own private iCloud storage. This includes your contacts, message and email text, notes, your conversations with Orbit, details about your photos, small face images, and the sign-ins for accounts you connect.
  • Orbit analyses your photos on your device and does not upload them. The photo details it syncs to your other devices include when and where a photo was taken and who appears in it.

What leaves your devices

  • AI answers (Cloud AI, on by default). To answer a question, Orbit sends it to Anthropic, the maker of Claude, with the data needed to answer it. That can include names, contact details, birthdays, cities, notes, calendar events, and excerpts of your messages and email. Orbit also sends voice-memo transcripts, and details of possible duplicate contacts about once a day. If you turn on transcription with Claude, journal page images are sent too.
  • Names, email addresses and phone numbers are sent as written unless you turn on Hide names in Security settings. The Read my messages setting (on by default) controls whether message and email text is included.
  • Orbit’s own connection uses Anthropic’s API. If you connect your own Claude subscription (on a Mac), your ChatGPT plan or your own Gemini API key, the same information goes to Anthropic, OpenAI or Google under your account, and that account’s terms apply, including whether the provider may use it to improve its models. On Google’s free tier, Google may use it to improve its products and its reviewers may read it.
  • Turning Cloud AI off in Security settings stops all of the above.
  • Profile details. When you choose Scrape Data for LinkedIn or Instagram, Orbit sends your contacts’ profile links, which include their usernames, to Bright Data, which returns their public profile details. Orbit then downloads profile pictures from the addresses it returns. After you import your LinkedIn data, Orbit also checks your own LinkedIn profile this way about twice a day; pausing LinkedIn in Orbit stops this.
  • Places. To show places on a map, Orbit sends place names and coordinates to Apple’s location service: your location when Find My is connected, your contacts’ cities, and photo locations in the photo browser. Higher security levels in Orbit stop sending your location and your contacts’ cities.
  • Messages to us. If you use Message the Developer, Orbit sends your message with your app version, system version, device model, and language and region, encrypted so that only we can read it.
  • Debugging (off by default). If you turn on “Send chats & data for debugging”, Orbit sends us your conversations with Orbit, including the data used to answer them, and from a Mac a full copy of your Orbit database about every 36 hours, encrypted so that only we can read it. Orbit removes these from Apple’s servers once we have collected them, or after 30 days. Turning the switch off stops sending.
  • Updates. The Mac app checks GitHub for new versions.
  • Accounts you connect are read with your own sign-in, directly from the service.

Retention and deletion

Orbit keeps your data until you delete it; it does not expire. Voice memos are deleted once Orbit has read them, or on the schedule you choose.

Disconnecting an account stops Orbit reading it and removes its sign-in from this device and from your iCloud copy. Data already imported stays on your devices.

“Delete Relayed Data Now”, under Pair Your Devices, deletes Orbit’s copy in your iCloud. Deleting Orbit from an iPhone removes its database from that iPhone; removing Orbit from a Mac does not remove its data folder. Neither deletes the copy on your other devices or in your iCloud.

Data sent to Anthropic, OpenAI, Google or Bright Data is kept under their terms. You can revoke Orbit’s access to Google at myaccount.google.com/permissions; disconnecting in Orbit does not do this for you.
